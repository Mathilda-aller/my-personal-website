# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

The site has two audiences without splitting into two personalities. Recruiters and collaborators should discover evidence of product and engineering ability through the work itself. Friends and personal visitors should feel that they have entered Mathilda's private visual world: photographs, movement, food, language, music, and small observations.

## Product Purpose

Create an English-language personal website that works as both a portfolio and a living visual diary. Anima Isle is the flagship work, but the site must never feel like a résumé template. Professional credibility should emerge naturally from thoughtful writing, project decisions, and finished artifacts.

## Brand Personality

Intimate, dreamy, spirited, and sincere. The voice is warm and lightly playful, with room for softness and unfinished thoughts. It should feel handmade and personal rather than optimized, corporate, or generated.

## Anti-references

Avoid résumé dashboards, oversized corporate typography, rigid twelve-column case studies, metric strips, equal project cards, SaaS landing pages, productivity-app aesthetics, and interfaces that could belong to any AI-generated portfolio. Do not replace the original photography, Pacifico handwriting, blue atmosphere, or emotional informality.

## Design Principles

1. Preserve before improving: original backgrounds, photography, handwriting, and emotional tone are source material, not technical debt.
2. Let work be discovered: professional evidence should appear through stories and artifacts rather than résumé furniture.
3. Compose like memory: allow layering, drifting alignment, marginal notes, and uneven rhythm.
4. Keep reading effortless: Pacifico is for short titles only; all long English copy uses a highly legible text face.
5. Stay unmistakably personal: if a section could be transferred unchanged to another portfolio, redesign it.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Maintain WCAG 2.2 AA readability, keyboard navigation, touch-friendly controls, useful alternative text, reduced-motion support, and clear form feedback. Expressive backgrounds must always be paired with sufficient text contrast.
